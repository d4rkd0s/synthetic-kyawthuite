# Laboratory Research Plan — synthetic kyawthuite

**Revision:** 0.1 planning proposal; NOT an established experimental SOP.

## Research question

Can independent laboratories reproducibly synthesize single-crystal BiSbO₄ with the same structure as natural kyawthuite and then optimize optical quality and useful crystal size, with procedures and results published openly?

**Distinguish:** correct elemental stoichiometry ≠ correct oxidation states ≠ correct crystal structure ≠ usable single crystal ≠ facetable gem.

## Starting reference

Natural kyawthuite: monoclinic I2/c, a=5.4624(4) Å, b=4.88519(17) Å, c=11.8520(8) Å, β=101.195(7)°, V=310.25(3) Å³, Z=4 (Kampf et al., 2017). Reference numbers refer to a *natural specimen* and must not be silently treated as universal acceptance tolerances.

## Work packages

### WP0 — primary-source recovery and safety feasibility
- Retrieve full Ito et al. (1975), Enjalbert et al. (1995), and Popolitov (1996) papers cited by Kampf et al. (2017); record exact titles, journal data, languages, precursor identity, vessel design, atmosphere, mineralizer/flux, pressure, temperature/time program, dimensions, and characterization.
- Identify any errata, polymorphs, later replication studies, crystal-structure refinements, hazards, and intellectual-property constraints.
- Produce a traceable evidence table: **reported**, **independently verified**, **unverified**, **proposed**.
- Ask a qualified laboratory to approve a facility-specific risk assessment and SOP before any experiment.
**Gate 0:** original procedural details and facility safety/capability reviewed. Without this, no experiments.

### WP1 — phase and characterization baselines
- Agree on quality-controlled reference material, instrumentation and measurement plan.
- Determine polymorph/phase using powder XRD; use single-crystal XRD when appropriate.
- Check Bi:Sb stoichiometry and impurities by calibrated elemental analysis; oxygen content/oxidation state may require complementary specialized measurements.
- Obtain Raman spectra and optical observations under documented conditions.
**Gate 1:** structure/chemistry confirmed and raw data archived.

### WP2 — reproduce reported crystal growth
- **Hydrothermal, primary candidate:** reconstruct Popolitov (1996) under qualified high-pressure laboratory supervision. Reported range 450–520°C and maximum crystal size 2 × 4 × 18 mm, but **pressure, chemistry, equipment, and hold times remain unverified here**.
- **Vapor transport/growth:** evaluate Ito et al. (1975); cited growth at 900–1200°C with crystals up to 1 mm × 14 mm. Transport mechanism and operating details must be confirmed from source.
- **Flux, alternative:** evaluate Enjalbert et al. (1995); cited Bi₂O₃–Sb₂O₃–V₂O₅ flux, ~125 μm crystals at 800°C. Verify potential flux incorporation and disposal plans.
- **Powder reference/control:** literature-backed solid-state synthesis considered separately, not a proxy for a single crystal.
**Gate 2:** at least one independently characterized single crystal from a documented repeatable procedure; compare across runs and labs.

### WP3 — systematic optimization
- After a safe SOP exists, laboratory designs a controlled study of documented parameters (e.g., composition, temperature profile, mineralizer/flux, gradients, seed usage, growth time) as appropriate to the method.
- Record morphology, dimensions, cracks, inclusions, phase purity, growth yield, transparency, color, and contamination. Include negative results.
- Replicate most promising conditions with a second operator/lab where possible.
**Gate 3:** measurable improvement with repeatability, not anecdotal appearance alone.

### WP4 — optical and gem-quality evaluation
- Optical microscopy/transmission, spectroscopy, clarity/inclusions, cleavage and fracture handling; independent gemological testing if any material is to be faceted.
- Report finished facetable yield, not just maximum uncut dimensions.
**Gate 4:** independently described facetable material with transparent provenance.

### WP5 — publication and reproducibility
- Archive SOP versions, calibration, raw diffraction files, instrument metadata, lab notebooks, data processing, and failure analyses.
- Obtain institutional release authorization and publish datasets with persistent identifiers when feasible.
- Label synthesized material accurately; never market as natural kyawthuite.

## What not to claim
- No known success in this project yet.
- A literature temperature alone is **not** an SOP; high-temperature pressure vessels pose serious rupture and exposure hazards.
- A BiSbO₄ powder sample or diffraction match alone does not prove gem-quality crystal growth.
- A natural mineral being scarce does not imply synthetic BiSbO₄ cannot be made.

## Proposed deliverables
D0 evidence register + source PDFs/links (link licensed publications, do not rehost without permission).
D1 approved facility-specific safety review and protocol.
D2 verified powder/single-crystal XRD package and chemical analyses.
D3 replication dataset plus images and measurement logs.
D4 optimization report including failures.
D5 open methods manuscript and datasets.
