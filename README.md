# Open Synthetic Kyawthuite Project

**Open research toward reproducible synthetic kyawthuite (Bi³⁺Sb⁵⁺O₄) crystals.**

This public, laboratory-oriented project aims to collect primary literature, validate crystal chemistry, commission independent replication, and openly publish results from crystalline powder through optically useful single crystals. **No experimental synthesis has been performed or independently validated by this repository.**

## Why this is feasible

Kyawthuite is the naturally occurring monoclinic form of bismuth antimonate, BiSbO₄ (IMA 2015-078). Natural kyawthuite was described from one faceted 1.61-carat specimen from Mogok, Myanmar. **Synthetic BiSbO₄ predates its mineral identification**. The original mineral description cites vapor-grown crystals up to 1 mm × 14 mm (Ito et al., 1975), flux-grown crystals ~125 μm (Enjalbert et al., 1995), and hydrothermally grown crystals up to 2 × 4 × 18 mm (Popolitov, 1996). These reports demonstrate plausible laboratory paths; they do **not** constitute a complete independently verified recipe, proof of current repeatability, or proof of gem quality.

Read: [original mineral description](https://doi.org/10.1180/minmag.2016.080.102) | [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/9669t-pbb15) | [RRUFF-hosted paper](https://rruff.info/rruff_1.0/uploads/MM81_477.pdf).

## Project outcomes

1. **Evidence:** recover and verify primary crystal-growth publications, exact conditions, limitations, and provenance.
2. **Identity:** establish reference powder/single-crystal XRD and complementary Raman/compositional evidence against natural kyawthuite.
3. **Replication:** develop a lab-approved, safe, independently reproducible synthesis protocol.
4. **Crystal growth:** test hydrothermal, flux, and vapor approaches via qualified facilities; optimize dimensions, integrity, optical quality, and yield.
5. **Open release:** publish raw data, successful and failed runs, and versioned procedures with clear attribution.

## What laboratories are being asked to do

**First request: literature-based feasibility and protocol reconstruction** with equipment requirements, costs, hazards, go/no-go criteria, and availability of validated high-temperature/high-pressure equipment. No home synthesis or unsupervised pressure experiments are requested. The hydrothermal temperature range (450–520 °C) is historical literature context, **not** a recommendation to operate pressure equipment at these temperatures without an engineered procedure.
